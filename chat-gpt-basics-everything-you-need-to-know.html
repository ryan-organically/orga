The speed at which AI is progressing is terrifying. As these systems continue to evolve at breakneck pace, staying informed and adaptable is crucial.

The companies that thrive won't be those that simply deploy AI, but those that find the right balance between automation and human insight. Here, I'll break down everything from the basics to the "wait, it can do that?" aspects of Chat GPT, and why as a web developer and SEO marketer, I'm both thrilled and terrified by this tech.

What Does GPT Stand For?

Let's start with the basics. GPT stands for Generative Pre-trained Transformer.

Yeah, I know. Super helpful, right? Let me break that down:

  • Generative: It generates new content rather than just finding existing stuff
  • Pre-trained: It's been fed an ungodly amount of internet text before you ever typed a word to it
  • Transformer: The type of neural network architecture that powers it

In human terms please? It's a super-advanced text prediction system that's been trained on enough written content to make your high school English teacher have an existential crisis.

While the technical details might make your eyes glaze over (unless you're into that sort of thing), what matters is that GPT is crazy. It's the difference between asking Siri "What's the weather?" and having a virtual assistant that can write your college thesis, debug your code, or create marketing content that doesn't sound like it was written by a robot.

Who Owns Chat GPT?

Chat GPT is owned by OpenAI, a company that started as a non-profit research lab in 2015 but has since morphed into a for-profit company with a non-profit parent organization. Talk about an identity crisis.

OpenAI was founded by Sam Altman, Elon Musk (yes, that Elon Musk), and several others. Musk has since left the board, probably because he had too many companies to fire people thru email simultaneously.

He's since become a tad disgruntled about Sam's desired switch to for-profit as it threatens the child he pays most attention to: Grok. So he bid $97.4 billion for it. And Sam turned it down.

Microsoft has invested billions into OpenAI, which explains why Bing is now somewhat relevant again. It's at least still relevant for SEO. The Soft has bet the metaphorical farm on AI being the next big thing, and looking at the pace of advancement, it seems like a pretty solid bet.

As someone who works in the digital space, I've watched 'Chat' transform from "bro I just did my midterm paper in 4 minutes" to "holy shit, we're actually cooked" in a few months. The implications for web development and SEO are massive – which is why I've been diving deep into these tools.

Is Chat GPT Safe?

This is probably the question I get asked most often when I tell clients about integrating AI into their digital strategy. The short answer is: mostly yes, with some caveats.

Chat GPT itself doesn't have some secret agenda to steal your data and sell it to the highest bidder. OpenAI has privacy policies in place, but like any tech service, you should assume anything you type might not be 100% private.

The bigger safety concerns come in a few flavors:

  1. Content safety: Early versions had a tendency to make things up (AI hallucinations) or occasionally produce harmful content. The guardrails have improved dramatically, but it's not perfect.
  2. Job displacement: If you're a content writer, copywriter, or work in similar fields, there's legitimate concern about AI's impact on job markets. As someone who creates websites and handles SEO strategies, I've had to adapt my skillset to incorporate AI rather than compete with it. Everyone has to.
  3. Misinformation: Chat GPT can sometimes present incorrect information with absolute confidence. Always DYOR.
  4. Security concerns: If you're sharing sensitive business information, there's always some risk.
Chat GPT is safe in the sense that it won't hack your computer or steal your identity, but like any powerful tool, it needs to be used responsibly. As businesses rush to implement AI, we're seeing a spectrum from thoughtful integration to "let's replace our entire customer service team with a bot and pray."

How to Cite Chat GPT

If you're a student or researcher using Chat GPT for academic work, citing it properly is crucial unless you enjoy awkward conversations about academic integrity with your professor. We've all been there… No? Just me?

Most academic style guides have updated their recommendations to include AI tools. The general format is similar to citing a website, but includes the specific model used.

For APA style (7th edition), you would cite Chat GPT like this:

OpenAI. (2023). ChatGPT (Mar 14 version) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com

For MLA style (9th edition):

ChatGPT. Conversation with OpenAI's ChatGPT model. OpenAI, 14 Mar. 2023, chat.openai.com.

Keep in mind that most academic institutions recommend using Chat GPT as a supplementary resource rather than a primary source. It's more like asking a really smart friend for help than citing a peer-reviewed journal.

How to Use Chat GPT Effectively

Let's get into the good stuff. Using Chat GPT effectively is an art form – the difference between asking for a "logo" and getting clip art versus describing exactly what you want and getting something usable.

Here are my top tips for getting the most out of Chat GPT:

Be Specific and Detailed

The quality of your output depends directly on the quality of your input. Vague prompts get vague responses.

Bad prompt: "Write me a blog post about SEO."

Good prompt: "Write a 1000-word blog post about local SEO strategies for restaurants in small towns, including specific action items for Google Business Profile optimization and tips for getting reviews. Use a conversational tone with some humor."

Use the System to Your Advantage

Chat GPT retains context within a conversation, so you can refine and build on previous prompts. Start broad, then narrow down and refine.

Learn Prompt Engineering

This is where the real power users separate themselves from casual dabblers. Prompt engineering is essentially learning how to "talk" to AI to get exactly what you want.

Some techniques include:

  • Role prompting: "Act as an expert SEO consultant with 15 years of experience in the restaurant industry"
  • Format specification: "Present your answer as a bullet-point list with three sections"
  • Temperature adjustment: For more creative or diverse outputs, you can adjust this setting in the API (though not in the free web interface)

Verify and Edit

I cannot stress this enough – never take Chat GPT's output as gospel. It can hallucinate facts, make up statistics, and occasionally produce complete nonsense with supreme confidence.

Always fact-check important information and edit the output to match your voice and needs.

In my web development & SEO work, I use Chat GPT as a coding partner and efficiency booster, not as a replacement for my expertise. It helps me generate ideas, draft content outlines, and analyze data faster, but the final product still requires my human touch.

How Smart Is Chat GPT?

This question is harder to answer than it seems. Chat GPT isn't "smart" in the way humans are smart. It doesn't truly understand the world, have consciousness (yet), or possess what we'd recognize as knowledge or intelligence.

What it has is an extraordinary ability to predict text patterns based on massive amounts of training data. It's pattern recognition at a scale we've never seen before.

Chat GPT-4 can score in the 90th percentile on the Bar exam, write college-level essays, debug complex code, and translate between languages. But it can also confidently tell you that the sky is green if prompted in certain ways.

It's both more impressive and less intelligent than it seems. It can write a sonnet in Shakespeare's style but doesn't know what a sunset actually looks like. It can explain quantum physics but doesn't truly grasp what an atom is.

The most recent models like GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 Sonnet have made significant leaps forward. They feel almost human in their responses, which is both amazing and unsettling.

In fact, Anthropic just released Claude Opus 4.5 (November 2025), which is currently the most advanced frontier model available. While Chat GPT dominates the conversation in mainstream AI discussions, Claude Opus 4.5 represents a different philosophy – it's designed for complex reasoning tasks that require deep analysis and extended context windows. For developers and SEO professionals working with large codebases or comprehensive content strategies, Opus 4.5's ability to maintain coherent reasoning across 200,000+ tokens is a game-changer.

The AI landscape is no longer a one-horse race. While Chat GPT remains the household name, models like Opus 4.5 are pushing the boundaries in specialized domains. The smart play isn't picking a side – it's understanding which tool excels at which task.

Where Does Chat GPT Get Its Information?

Chat GPT gets its information from its training data – a massive collection of text from the internet, books, articles, and other sources that it was fed during development.

For GPT-3.5, this training data has a cutoff date, meaning it claims it doesn't know about events after that date unless they've been added to its system through additional training.

GPT-4 and newer models have more recent training data, but they still have knowledge cutoffs. They're not actively searching the web like Google does. Although there's some inconsistencies here.

This is a crucial limitation to understand: Chat GPT isn't browsing the internet to answer your questions in real-time. It's drawing on the patterns and information it learned during training.

However, newer features like Plugins and Browse with Bing allow some versions of Chat GPT to access current information and perform web searches, blurring this line somewhat.

Is Chat GPT Private?

Privacy is a legitimate concern with any AI tool. According to OpenAI, they save your conversations and may use them to further train their models – unless you opt out in your settings.

For the average user asking about dinner recipes or help with homework, this probably isn't a big deal. But if you're sharing business strategies or sensitive information, it's worth considering.

Some privacy considerations:

  • Free Chat GPT conversations are saved by default
  • OpenAI does have human reviewers who may look at conversations
  • Enterprise and business versions offer stronger privacy controls
  • You should avoid sharing personally identifiable information

I generally recommend treating Chat GPT like a public forum rather than a private conversation, especially for business use. Finding a balance between remaining vague but tailored to your situation is key.

Can Chat GPT Read PDFs?

Yes, Chat GPT can read PDFs with limitations. This functionality is available in ChatGPT Plus (the paid version) and through the API.

However, Chat GPT can only read the text content of PDFs, not complex tables or images (though that's changing with multimodal models). And there's a file size limit, so extremely large documents might need to be split up.

The process is pretty straightforward:

  1. Upload your PDF document
  2. Chat GPT will process and extract the text
  3. You can then ask questions about the content

This feature is incredibly useful for summarizing long documents, extracting specific information, analyzing reports or research papers, and converting PDF content into other formats.

Can Chat GPT Watch Videos?

The standard versions of Chat GPT cannot watch or analyze videos directly. However, newer multimodal models like GPT-4o can analyze static images and with the right plugins, can process some video content, though not with the same comprehension level as humans.

If you need to get information from video content into Chat GPT, your options are:

  1. Transcribe the video first, then upload the transcript
  2. Describe the key elements of the video manually
  3. Use specialized tools designed for video analysis, then share the results

As a web developer who sometimes needs to analyze video content for clients, I've found that the combination of separate transcription tools and then feeding that text to Chat GPT works reasonably well as a workaround. Puppeteering a collection of these tools in streamlined processes is a hot skill to have in 2025.

Can Chat GPT Transcribe Audio?

Similar to video, the standard Chat GPT doesn't have native audio transcription capabilities. However, OpenAI has released Whisper, an automatic speech recognition system that can transcribe audio with impressive accuracy.

While they haven't fully integrated Whisper into the Chat GPT web interface, OpenAI's API allows developers to use both technologies together. This means there are now third-party tools and plugins that can handle the audio transcription and then feed that text to Chat GPT.

How to Upload a File to Chat GPT

If you're using ChatGPT Plus or have API access, uploading files is relatively straightforward:

  1. Look for the paperclip or upload icon in the chat interface
  2. Select your file from your device
  3. Wait for the upload to complete
  4. Ask questions about the file's content, or give it a gigantic detailed prompt alongside the screenshot so it has all the context in one prompt

Currently, Chat GPT supports various file types including text files (.txt), PDFs, spreadsheets (CSV, XLSX), code files (Python, JavaScript, etc.), and some image formats (in newer versions).

How We Used AI to Research This Post: Keyword Purity Scoring

Here's where it gets meta. This entire blog post was strategically researched using a methodology I call the Keyword Purity Scoring System – a data-driven approach to identifying content opportunities that are both rankable AND convert.

As an SEO professional, I'm constantly looking for the sweet spot: keywords that are easy enough to rank for, have enough search demand to matter, and indicate buyer intent (high CPC means advertisers pay for these clicks).

The Keyword Purity Formula

The core formula weighs three critical factors:

Organic Purity = (Difficulty_Score × 0.35) + (CPC_Score × 0.35) + (Volume_Score × 0.30)

Before applying these weights, we normalize each component:

  • Difficulty_Score = (100 - Difficulty) → inverted, so lower difficulty = higher score
  • CPC_Score = (CPC / Max_CPC_in_dataset) × 100
  • Volume_Score = (Volume / Max_Volume_in_dataset) × 100

Why These Weights?

  • Difficulty: 35% – You can't benefit from a keyword you can't rank for
  • CPC: 35% – High CPC means advertisers pay for these clicks, indicating buyer intent
  • Volume: 30% – Demand matters but is a tiebreaker; high volume often correlates with high difficulty and low intent

The Tiering System

After scoring keywords, I categorize them into tiers:

  • Tier 1 (Pure Gold): Purity ≥ 60 – Prioritize these
  • Tier 2 (High Purity): Purity 45-59, Difficulty ≤ 20 – Quick wins
  • Tier 3 (Solid): Purity 35-44, Difficulty ≤ 30 – Supporting content

The beauty of this system? A low-volume, zero-difficulty, high-CPC keyword beats a high-volume, high-difficulty, low-CPC keyword. You can win the first one and it converts.

This methodology is repeatable across any niche. Export your keyword data from Ahrefs, SEMrush, or similar tools, run it through the scoring formula, and you have a ranked list of exactly where to focus your content efforts.
